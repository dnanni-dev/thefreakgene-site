The origin

Something you're born with.

“You've got the freak gene.” Said to Cass, once — and it stuck

Someone said it after hearing how Cass talks about sex: openly, without embarrassment, with real curiosity. As if it had to be inherited, because nobody learns to be that comfortable.

She took it as a compliment. For some people this way of thinking isn't a phase or a performance. It's just how they're wired. But being wired for curiosity doesn't mean being careless with it. Openness carries responsibility: to your partners, to yourself, and to the truth about what this actually looks like.

The Freak Gene is where that responsibility lives. Cass is studying to become a sexologist, and this is her space to teach, to talk and to share what she learns.

i.

Natural, not forced

Desire is personal. Nobody should be pushed toward it or shamed out of it. You get to discover what's true for you, at your own pace.

ii.

Curious, not careless

Questions are welcome. Assumptions aren't. Every exploration starts with information, communication and a clear yes.

iii.

Respect is the point

Kink isn't a costume or a plot device. It's real people with real trust between them, and it deserves to be treated that way.

Safe practice

The foundations, first.

Before anything else, these. They're the difference between exploration and harm, whatever you're into.

Consent

Every time, every step

F
Freely given
R
Reversible, at any point
I
Informed
E
Enthusiastic
S
Specific

Negotiation

Talk before you touch

Discuss desires, hard limits, soft limits, health and emotional triggers ahead of time, when everyone is clear-headed. A yes to one thing is never a yes to everything.

Safewords

A way out, always

Green: all good, keep going
Yellow: slow down, check in
Red: stop, completely

Agree on a non-verbal signal too, for when words aren't possible.

Risk awareness

Know what you're doing

Some activities carry real physical risk. Learn the specifics, start slowly, never mix in alcohol or drugs, and stay within your actual skill level.

Check-ins

Keep the conversation open

Bodies and feelings change in the moment. Asking "how are you doing?" isn't a mood-killer. It's what makes trust possible.

Aftercare

The part that matters most

Comfort, water, warmth, reassurance, talking it through, for everyone involved. Plan it in advance, and follow up the next day too.
